1. Project Overview
1.1 Introduction
The E-Commerce Sales & Customer Analytics project is an end-to-end data analytics project focused on analyzing e-commerce transaction data to understand sales performance, customer behavior, product performance, revenue patterns, and customer value.

The project follows a complete analytics workflow starting from raw transactional data and progressing through data quality inspection, data cleaning, Python-based analysis, SQL business analysis, RFM customer segmentation, and interactive Power BI dashboard development.

The objective is to transform large-scale transactional data into structured business insights that can support sales analysis, customer analysis, product performance analysis, and business decision-making.
1.2 Project Objectives
The main objectives are:
- Analyze overall sales performance.
- Understand revenue and order trends.
- Identify top-performing products.
- Analyze customer purchasing behavior.
- Understand repeat purchasing behavior.
- Measure customer revenue contribution.
- Identify valuable customer segments.
- Analyze customer retention and risk.
- Understand geographic sales performance.
- Identify time-based sales patterns.
- Develop interactive dashboards for business analysis.
1.3 Business Context
E-commerce businesses generate large volumes of transactional data containing information about orders, products, customers, dates, prices, quantities, and locations.

However, raw transactional data does not directly provide business insights.

The data needs to be inspected, cleaned, transformed, analyzed, and visualized to support informed business decisions.

This project uses Python, SQL, RFM analysis, and Power BI to convert raw transaction data into meaningful business insights.

7. RFM Methodology
7.1 Overview
RFM analysis was used to evaluate customer behavior and identify meaningful customer segments based on purchasing activity.

RFM stands for:
- Recency
- Frequency
- Monetary
The analysis was performed at the customer level using transactions with valid customer identification.
7.2 RFM Dimensions
Recency

Recency measures how recently a customer made a purchase.

It is calculated as the number of days between the customer's most recent purchase date and the analysis reference date.

A lower number of recency days indicates a more recent purchase.

Frequency

Frequency measures how often a customer made purchases.

In this project, frequency is based on the number of distinct invoices or orders associated with each customer.

A higher frequency indicates that the customer has purchased more frequently.

Monetary

Monetary measures the total revenue generated by a customer.

It is calculated by summing transaction revenue for each identified customer.

A higher monetary value indicates greater customer revenue contribution.
7.3 RFM Scoring
Each RFM dimension was converted into a score from 1 to 5.

The scoring system divides customers into five groups using quantile-based scoring.

Recency scoring
- R5 = Most recent customers.
- R1 = Least recent customers.
Customers with lower recency days receive higher scores.

Frequency scoring
- F1 = Lowest purchase frequency.
- F5 = Highest purchase frequency.
Higher frequency receives a higher score.

Monetary scoring

Monetary scores range from M1 to M5. Customers with higher monetary values receive higher scores.
7.4 RFM Segmentation
The RFM scores are combined to understand customer purchasing behavior and assign customers to meaningful segments.

The segmentation supports analysis of:
- High-value customers.
- Loyal customers.
- Potentially valuable customers.
- Customers requiring attention.
- At-risk customers.
- Customers with low purchasing activity.
7.5 Business Importance
RFM analysis helps businesses understand customer value and purchasing behavior.

It supports customer retention, targeted marketing, customer engagement, and revenue improvement strategies.

11. Project Limitations
11.1 Missing Customer IDs
A significant number of transaction records do not contain a Customer ID.

Therefore, the project uses two analytical layers:
- Transaction Layer: Complete cleaned transaction dataset for revenue, orders, units, product, geographic, and time analysis.
- Customer Layer: Transactions with valid Customer IDs for customer-level analysis, RFM, segmentation, and customer risk analysis.
Customer-level results should not be interpreted as representing every transaction.
11.2 Historical Dataset
The analysis is based on historical transaction data covering December 2009 to December 2011.

The findings should not automatically be assumed to represent current business conditions.
11.3 No Profitability Data
The dataset contains sales transaction information but does not provide complete business profitability information.

The project focuses on revenue and sales activity rather than profit or profit margin.
11.4 No Marketing Cost Data
The dataset does not contain detailed advertising spend, campaign costs, customer acquisition cost, conversion rates, or marketing channel information.

Therefore, marketing return on investment cannot be determined.
11.5 No Inventory Information
The dataset does not provide complete inventory information.

The project cannot directly determine current stock levels, stock-outs, reorder points, inventory carrying costs, or supply constraints.
11.6 No Customer Demographic Information
The dataset does not provide customer age, gender, occupation, household income, education, or detailed preferences.

Customer segmentation is based primarily on purchasing behavior.
11.7 RFM Is Behavioral Segmentation
RFM describes historical purchasing behavior. It does not directly explain why a customer stopped purchasing, customer satisfaction, customer preferences, competitor activity, or external market conditions.
11.8 RFM Reference Date
The RFM analysis uses the reference date 2011-12-10 to calculate customer recency.

Changing the reference date can change recency values, scores, and customer segments.
11.9 Customer Segment Rules
The RFM customer segments are created using defined combinations of RFM scores.

Examples include:
- Champions.
- Loyal Customers.
- At Risk.
- Needs Attention.
- Hibernating.
- Potential Loyalists.
- Big Spenders.
- Recent Customers.
These are analytical classifications based on the project's defined rules, not machine-learning predictions.
11.10 Customer Value Thresholds
Customer value analysis uses revenue thresholds to classify customers into groups such as VIP, High, Medium, and Low.

These are business rules and can change if the thresholds are modified.
11.11 At-Risk Classification Is Not a Prediction Model
The At Risk segment is derived from RFM characteristics.

It does not estimate churn probability, future purchases, customer lifetime value, or future revenue.
11.12 Geographic Analysis Limitations
The dataset includes country information, but detailed geographic information such as city, region, postal code, delivery location, and store location is not used as a complete geographic hierarchy.

The analysis primarily focuses on country-level performance.
11.13 Time Analysis Limitations
The project analyzes year, month, day of week, and hour.

It does not incorporate holidays, weather, competitor promotions, economic conditions, marketing campaigns, or other external events.
11.14 No Causal Analysis
The project identifies patterns, relationships, concentrations, customer segments, and sales trends.

It does not establish causal relationships.
11.15 No Real-Time Data
The Power BI dashboard is based on the finalized project dataset. It is not a real-time operational monitoring system.
11.16 No Predictive Analytics
The current project focuses on descriptive analytics, diagnostic analysis, customer segmentation, and business intelligence.

It does not currently implement predictive models for churn, sales forecasting, customer lifetime value, or product demand.
11.17 No Automated Data Refresh Pipeline
The project does not include a fully automated production pipeline that continuously extracts, cleans, loads, analyzes, and refreshes new data.
